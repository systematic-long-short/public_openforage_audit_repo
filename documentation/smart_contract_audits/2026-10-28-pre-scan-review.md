## A14-10 and the no-self-veto rule
The design gives a guardian no cancel or veto over its own authority. The 2026-10-11 round tried three times to narrow the A14-10 protection, and each review rejected the attempt: each limited the protection to a rotation of the cancelling guardian's own seat, which let one guardian cancel a proposal that rotates another. This change keeps the protection for every seat and drops it only while the rotation cannot execute:
- An accelerated rotation runs through `executeAcceleratedRotation`, which any allowed caller can call once four guardians have approved and the 10-minute floor has passed. Cancelling a governance proposal that carries it never stops the rotation.
- A routine rotation is finalized only by the Timelock, and only after the 8-day routine rotation delay. A guardian can cancel a finalize proposal made before the delay ends; a finalize proposed after it is protected. Routine rotations do not expire, so this is a delay, never a veto.
- Only the current Timelock can change a pre-commit (`setPreCommittedSuccessor`), so no guardian can remove the protection by changing one, and both execution paths refuse a rotation whose pre-commit no longer matches.
A local check rotates the seat of the guardian who then tries to cancel, and passes all four cases:
- with a ready accelerated rotation, the cancel is refused (`SelfTargetingGuardianMutation`);
- with an unready one, the cancel succeeds, and once the rotation is ready an unrelated allowed caller executes it directly: the guardian's permissions fall to zero;
- with a routine finalize proposed after the delay, the cancel is refused;
- with a routine finalize proposed before the delay, the cancel succeeds, and the same finalize proposed again after the delay is refused cancellation.
## The StakingQueueModule split
`StakingQueueModule` had 65 bytes left under EIP-170 in the Deploy profile, too few for the queue fixes. `StakingQueueModule.sol` now holds an abstract `StakingQueueModuleStorage` (the storage mirror, types and shared helpers) and the core module (join, cancel and process). Tier upgrades, reversions, expired lockups, tier-vault sync and unlock retries moved to the new `StakingQueueLockupModule`, reached through the core's fallback by a second delegatecall over the same storage. The deployment script deploys the lockup module and checks the wiring (`openforage_smart_contracts/script/Deploy.s.sol:605-606,1120-1123`). This is the delegatecall-module pattern of the earlier Treasury and Governor splits, with no new storage and no change of behavior; the core now has 5,488 bytes left. OpenForage's private storage-layout checker confirms that the lockup module's layout equals the core's; that checker is not in the public tree.
## Remaining bounds
Removed by this update: the 40-epoch catch-up revert, the 1-9 raw-unit return-cap edge, the queue-cost limit, and the broad guardian-cancel protection. What remains:
- The weekly-exit reservation rule and its limits stand as recorded on 2026-10-11, including the conservative one-raw-unit sentinel after a full loss.
- Expired-proposal cleanup handles one Timelock operation ID at a time.
- Classification-only policy checks keep the static-call budgets measured on 2026-10-11; whole-scan gas is unmeasured.
- A proposer can still attach the finalize call of a routine rotation that has passed its delay and is not yet finalized. Governance removes that shield by finalizing the rotation, and a ready accelerated rotation's shield ends as soon as anyone executes it.
- A guardian removed and later re-added can still have an earlier approval counted for the same pending rotation until that rotation expires; a rotation that never becomes ready now expires 30 days after its proposal.
- The queue's 100 RISKUSD entry minimum and 8-entry cap are fixed in the code.
- New off-chain duties: after a profit attestation the attestor should recognise promptly, or recognise zero, because tier admissions wait until it does; a reindex activation or cancellation needs its `verifyVestingSourceAllowlists` pages run first; `RegisterAllowlist` needs `EXPECTED_CHAIN_ID`.
These are source dispositions only. They do not change an Octane finding status or establish runtime, deployed-state, or legacy-proxy behavior.
