## The weekly-exit rule

Each cohort's approved amount is reserved once against the next week's exit budget. A holder may collect that reservation only during that next calendar week. If a freeze or pause covers that whole week, the approval lapses and the holder must request again. A later settlement cannot restore a lapsed reservation. A budget change applies to the next cohort paid and never cuts an existing reservation; when share-value movement makes equal shares exceed the budget, the strict weekly limit wins.

## Remaining bounds for these findings

- Weekly exit reservations use the collection window's saved cap. A freeze or pause across that full calendar week lapses the approval; a later settlement gives no allocation, and a caller must trigger settlement. Unfilled shares return. Claim order can matter when share value moves; the strict weekly cap prevails over exactly equal shares in that case. The owner-set cap defaults to 500 basis points, and higher configured values remain published behavior.
- Queue catch-up handles at most 40 missed epochs. More than 40 makes that deposit revert.
- A 1-9 raw-unit lifetime return-cap basis rounds the 10% cap to zero.
- Expired-proposal cleanup handles one Timelock operation ID at a time.
- Classification-only policy checks retain static-call budgets measured at up to 1,515,000,000 gas for a bounded case and 3,014,850,000 gas for the concrete 199-candidate case. Whole-scan gas is unmeasured.
- Guardian rotation protection can leave a guardian unable to cancel a proposal rotating another seat.
- With no contract change, a guardian removed and later re-added can have an earlier approval counted again for the same pending rotation, but only until that rotation expires.
- Repeated small queue entries can use bounded keeper work and delay other entries; there is no per-entry minimum, per-user limit, or whole-call gas measurement.
- The weekly snapshot's one-raw-unit sentinel is conservative after a full loss.
